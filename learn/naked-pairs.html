Naked Pair

Two squares in a row, column or house with the same two notes and no others, so those numbers can't go anywhere else there.

01Worked Example 02Triples and Quads

Worked example

Naked Pair in Action

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for two squares in the same row, column or house with the same two notes and nothing else.
  2. 2Those two numbers fill those two squares, one each, though you can't tell yet which way round.
  3. 3Cross both numbers off every other square in that row, column or house.
  4. 4If the two squares share a house as well as a row or column, cross them off the house too.

What it does

Crosses notes off. Pairs are common, and often leave a single behind.

Triples and Quads

Three squares whose notes hold only three numbers between them work the same way, and so do four with four. Not every square needs every number: squares with 1 and 2, 2 and 3, and 1 and 3 make a Naked Triple.

Naked Triple

Intermediate

Three squares in a row, column or house whose notes, between them, hold only three numbers, so those numbers can't go anywhere else there.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for three squares in the same row, column or house whose notes, between them, hold only three numbers.
  2. 2Not every square needs all three numbers: squares with 1 and 2, 2 and 3, and 1 and 3 still make a Naked Triple.
  3. 3Those three numbers fill those three squares, one each, so cross all three off every other square in that row, column or house.
  4. 4If all three squares share a house as well as a row or column, cross them off the house too.

What it does

Crosses notes off. Triples are less common than pairs, and can leave a single behind.

Naked Quad

Advanced

Four squares in a row, column or house whose notes, between them, hold only four numbers, so those numbers can't go anywhere else there.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for four squares in the same row, column or house whose notes, between them, hold only four numbers.
  2. 2Not every square needs all four numbers; what matters is that no other number appears among the four squares.
  3. 3Those four numbers fill those four squares, one each, so cross all four off every other square in that row, column or house.

What it does

Crosses notes off. Quads are rare, and usually turn up only on harder puzzles.

Putting it to work

Key Tips for Naked Sets

Recognition Patterns

  • Look for squares with only two to four notes
  • Check whether a few squares share the same notes
  • Count how many numbers their notes hold between them
  • There should be as many numbers as squares

Application Strategy

  • Start with naked pairs. They're the easiest to spot
  • Focus on rows, columns and houses with plenty of notes
  • Check rows, columns and houses alike
  • Cross the notes off as soon as you find a set
