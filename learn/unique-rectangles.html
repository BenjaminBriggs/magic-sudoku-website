Unique Rectangle Type 1

Four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, three with only the same two notes: the fourth can't be either number, or the puzzle would have two answers.

Worked example

Unique Rectangle Type 1 in Action

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for squares with only the same two notes, in two rows and two columns, across two houses.
  2. 2If three corners of such a rectangle have only those two notes, look at the fourth.
  3. 3If the fourth were either number, the two numbers could swap round the rectangle, and the puzzle would have two answers.
  4. 4So cross both numbers off the fourth corner.

What it does

Crosses notes off. It relies on the puzzle having only one answer, as every proper puzzle does.

Type 2

When two corners on one side have only the pair, and the other two each have the same one extra number, one of those two must be that number. Cross it off any square that sees both.

Unique Rectangle Type 2

Master

Four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two on one side have only the same two notes and the other two have those plus the same one extra number. One of those two must be that number, or the puzzle would have two answers, so any square that sees both can't.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two corners on one side have only the same two notes.
  2. 2Check that the other two corners each have those two notes plus the same one extra number.
  3. 3One of those two corners must hold that extra number, or the two numbers could swap corners and the puzzle would have two answers.
  4. 4Cross that extra number off any square that sees both corners.

Type 3

When two corners on one side have only the pair, the other two corners' extra notes act like one square. With other squares in a row, column or house they share, they can make a set, such as a Naked Pair. Cross the set's numbers off the rest of it.

Unique Rectangle Type 3

Master

Four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two on one side have only the same two notes. The other two corners' extra notes act together like one square, and make a set with other squares in a row, column or house they share, so the set's numbers can be crossed off the rest of it.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two corners on one side have only the same two notes.
  2. 2Look at the extra notes in the other two corners. At least one must be placed there, so together the two corners act like one square.
  3. 3In a row, column or house those two corners share, find one or more other squares whose notes make a set with those extra notes, such as a Naked Pair.
  4. 4Cross the set's numbers off every other square in that row, column or house.

Type 4

When two corners on one side have only the pair, and one of its numbers can only go in the other two corners of a row, column or house they share, one of them must be that number. If the other were the pair's second number, the two numbers could swap corners, so cross the second number off both.

Unique Rectangle Type 4

Master

Four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two on one side have only the same two notes. If one of those numbers can only go in the other two corners of a row, column or house they share, the other number can be crossed off both.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two corners on one side have only the same two notes.
  2. 2Look at a row, column or house the other two corners share, and check whether one of the pair's two numbers can only go in those two corners.
  3. 3One of them must be that number. If the other were the pair's second number, the two numbers could swap corners and the puzzle would have two answers.
  4. 4Cross the pair's second number off both corners.

Type 5

When two opposite corners, or three, have the pair plus the same one extra number, one of them must be that number. Cross it off any square that sees them all.

Unique Rectangle Type 5

Master

Four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses, where two opposite corners, or three, have the same two notes plus the same one extra number, and the rest have only the two. One of them must be that number, so any square that sees them all can't.

How to spot it

  1. 1Look for four squares at the corners of a rectangle across two houses whose notes all hold the same two numbers.
  2. 2Check that two opposite corners, or three corners, have those two notes plus the same one extra number, and the rest have only the two.
  3. 3One of those corners must hold that extra number, or the two numbers could swap corners and the puzzle would have two answers.
  4. 4Cross that extra number off any square that sees them all.
